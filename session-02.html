Where session one stopped — exact state

  • Tactical: loading bay. Two Ashline pushing in from the service corridor. The Clicker is at the roll-up door, which is coming down. A PC threw gravel to scramble its echolocation and it is currently chasing the sound.
  • Objective: roof done — antenna feed spliced, Ellis's note recovered. Two full jerry cans in hand. Only the transmitter room is left, and they have the code.
  • Boone: not met. The booth is still an unopened door. Everything that scene was going to be is still available — and it's now a different scene entirely.
  • The body: the man in the loading bay was FEDRA, collecting an ammunition consignment from the Ashline. The party knows. That knowledge is the engine of this whole session.

Cold open Pick up on the throw

The gravel goes out in a spray and lands somewhere behind the pallet racks, and the sound it makes is a small, cheap, brilliant lie.

The Clicker's head snaps ninety degrees away from you. It shrieks at the rack — at nothing — and goes for it, arms wide.

Behind you the roll-up door is still coming down. Four feet. Three and a half.

And in the corridor, boots. Two of them, moving fast, and a voice shouting a name that isn't any of yours.

Everyone gets one thing. Go.

Rule the gravel throw properly — this is the most important thing you'll do all session

A player invented a tactic the book doesn't have. Reward it by making it real and repeatable, and the whole table starts inventing.

Sound bait (house rule). Any character may spend their Action to throw a scavenged object. Roll Aiming vs DC 7. On a success the target Infected moves to the sound and loses its next action; every survivor gets advantage on Elusive rolls against it for one round. Against a Clicker, add: it cannot re-acquire until it has physically reached the noise. On a critical, it stays confused an extra round. On a natural 1, it works out the throw came from you.

Announce this out loud as "the thing Dave did last session, it's a move now." Name it after the player.

The Door

Combat · timed

Three rounds until the roll-up door closes. Everything depends on which side of it people end up on.

The clock

  • Round 1 — door at 3½ ft. Crawlable at a cost (Elusive DC 6, prone on the far side).
  • Round 2 — 2 ft. Elusive DC 9 to roll under.
  • Round 3 — shut. Maintenance DC 10 at the chain hoist jams it open at any point.

Enemies

  • Clicker — 35–50 HP, 3D12, Sneak DC 10. Currently baited. It fights whatever it reaches first, Ashline included.
  • 2 × Ashline — Ruffian (bat, 1D10, 15 HP) and Robber (pipe, 2D10, 25 HP, Counter DC 8). They came for the gunshot, not for the party.

The good ending

Clicker outside, party inside, door shut. Let them buy it with a Maintenance roll or a clever throw rather than a fight — and let the two Ashline realise, mid-swing, that they are now locked in a room with five armed strangers and no Clicker to blame it on.

Those two can surrender. If anyone offers, take it. It's the fastest road to Boone.

0:2530 min

Boone, and the Body

Social · the pivot

Boone comes down because of the gunfire. He gets three steps into the loading bay, sees the dead man on the concrete, and stops.

"...Oh, you stupid, stupid people. Do you know what that is? No. No, you don't, or you'd be a lot quieter."

This is no longer a negotiation over territory. The party killed the reason the Ashline are still alive. Staff Sergeant Danny Ocampo drove up here every six weeks to collect brass and look the other way. When he doesn't come back, FEDRA sends twenty men who don't.

What Boone needs

  • The body gone or explained
  • His people off this ridge inside a day
  • Someone else to be holding the bag

He is frightened, and frightened is the most cooperative he will ever be. Play him fast and clipped, not menacing.

What the party can offer

  • Stage it as Infected. Medicine DC 11 to make the wounds read right. Fails badly if anyone shot him.
  • Carry him out and dump him a valley away. Costs an hour they may not have.
  • Burn the station. Ends the campaign objective. Let them consider it anyway.
  • Take the blame — walk away and let the Ashline point at strangers. Cheap now, expensive later.

What they actually want from him

They already have the transmitter code, so the key is worthless. What Boone has that nothing else does is two years of sitting on this ridge with a radio.

He has heard 1020. Once, about a year ago, a man's voice reading what sounded like a shipping forecast. He assumed it was nothing. Ask him and he'll tell you which way the antenna was pointed.

If they killed Boone in the fight

Then his second, Marisol, does this scene instead — angrier, less useful, and she wants the party gone rather than helping. She still knows about 1020, but they'll have to trade for it.

Four Nine One One

Puzzle · fast

They have everything. Let this be quick and competent — the reward for good play last session is that this part just works.

The sequence

  • Code on the keypad. The door has a pressure seal — it sighs when it opens. That's not a radio station door.
  • Two cans into the generator. Maintenance DC 10 for the seized starter.
  • Spliced feed connects at the rack.

What they see before they throw the switch

Behind a plywood panel bearing a sun-faded placard — KVLR · SERVING THE NORTH COAST SINCE 1961 — the equipment rack is olive drab. Property tags. A combination lock cast into the frame. A stencil across the top of the rack:

DESTROY BEFORE ABANDONMENT

Instinct or Maintenance DC 8: this gear was never commercial. It's twenty years older than it should be and built to a military spec.

1:1525 min

The Numbers

Reveal · the centrepiece

They throw the switch expecting to talk. The station talks first.

The generator catches. The rack lights come up one bank at a time, orange, and somewhere inside it a reel begins to turn — not because anyone told it to, but because it has been waiting eleven years for power and it knows exactly what it is for.

A woman's voice comes out of the monitor speaker. Recorded. Flat. Unhurried. American.

"Nine. Four. Seven. Two. One.
Nine. Four. Seven. Two. One.
Message follows. Message follows."

And then five-digit groups, and then more of them, in a voice that has not aged and does not care that everyone who was supposed to be listening is dead.

What KVLR was

A numbers station. A licensed low-power AM broadcaster that played country records to the North Coast, and whose real job was to carry one-way encrypted traffic on a schedule. The music was the cover. The tower, the power, the road and the staff were the point.

The party did not turn on a radio station. They reactivated a node.

Why Sundial answers

Because the stored routine transmitted a valid authentication preamble — and someone has been waiting six years for one. The party didn't find Sundial.

They said the password by accident.

Sundial, on 1490

"UNIDENTIFIED STATION ON ONE FOUR NINE ZERO. THIS IS SUNDIAL ACTUAL. YOUR AUTHENTICATION IS ELEVEN YEARS EXPIRED. IDENTIFY BY NAME, RANK AND UNIT. YOU HAVE SIXTY SECONDS."

Let the sixty seconds be real. Count them at the table.

Then, after the silence — same voice, but the procedure has gone out of it:

"...There isn't anybody left who knows the response. Is there."

Then Wren breaks in

Frantic, on the same frequency, from three ridges away — because she has been listening on a receiver and she just heard all of it. She tells them to get off the air and get to her. She sounds afraid of the wrong thing, and a sharp player will notice.

1:4025 min

Off the Ridge

Pressure · not a fight

Ocampo's radio check-in was due an hour ago. Headlights come up the ridge road — two vehicles, unhurried, which is worse than fast.

The clock

  • They cannot take the gate. It's locked and it's where the road is.
  • Boone's bolt-hole — cut fence behind the dumpsters, if the body scene went well
  • The roof plank to the maintenance shed — Elusive DC 9
  • The culvert under the lot, Survival DC 8, comes out in the creek draw

The hard question

Do they shut the transmitter off?

Leaving it running means the chain stays lit and Wren gets her bearing — and it means FEDRA drives up to a station that is loudly, obviously live, with Ashline in it.

Don't push them either way. Note what they choose. It decides who FEDRA hangs this on.

What they should see leaving

From the culvert or the shed roof, looking back: the tower, and one red lamp near the top that has not been lit since before some of them were born.

It is genuinely beautiful and it is a target.

2:0525 min

The Plantation

Fireflies · payment

Nine miles down the Nestucca drainage, in a stand of trees that has no business existing, the Firefly cell keeps a camp of eight people in a collapsed Forest Service guard station.

Who's there

  • Wren — courier. Freelancing, and about to be found out.
  • Hollis — cell lead, fifties, tired, did not authorise any of this and is going to say so.
  • Nadia — quartermaster and mechanic. Runs the bench. Warm, entirely transactional, the only one glad to see them.

The confrontation

They are carrying Ellis Vane's notebook. Wren recognises the handwriting from across the room.

She sent Ellis. He stopped answering eleven months ago. She never told anyone, because Hollis would have closed the file — and then she sent five strangers up the same road without mentioning him.

Her defence is thin and true: she didn't know he was dead, only that he was gone. Let her be bad at this.

Ends on

Nadia's set has been on 1490 since dark. Late in the scene it wakes up.

"STATION ON ONE FOUR NINE ZERO. WE HAVE YOUR BEARING. REMAIN AT YOUR POST."

The party is not at their post. But KVLR is still transmitting, and Boone is still in it.

Four numbers on a water-damaged page. Between Boone's two years of listening and Nadia's radio knowledge, the party can now work out that only one of these is an ordinary radio station — and it isn't the one they just switched on.

FreqEllis wroteWhat it actually is
532 kHzDarkBelow the broadcast band entirely. Nothing licensed lives here — it's maritime and military territory. "Dark" because whoever holds it only ever listens. This is Sundial's control frequency.
1020 kHz???? — 1 BroadcastAn ordinary clear-channel frequency. Ellis heard exactly one transmission and couldn't identify it. Boone heard it too, about a year ago — a man reading something like a shipping forecast. This is the next objective.
1452 kHz14XX — No AnswerNot a valid North American channel. The band runs on 10 kHz spacing — 1450 and 1460 exist, 1452 does not. It's a European allocation. Ellis crossed out his first guess because he knew the number was wrong and wrote it down anyway.
1490 kHzDC 4-9-1-1KVLR. One of only six shared "graveyard" local channels in the United States, crowded with hundreds of tiny low-power stations. The best place to hide a transmitter is in a crowd of identical ones.
Nadia's line, if a player pushes on 1452

"There's nothing at fourteen fifty-two. Not on this continent — we don't cut the band that way. Whatever your friend was hearing, it wasn't licensed here. It wasn't licensed anywhere near here."

Then move on. Do not explain it this year.

Setting Northern Oregon Coast Range

One correction worth making, because your players may know: coast redwoods stop at the Chetco River near Brookings, four hundred miles south of Tillamook. Northern Oregon is Sitka spruce, Douglas fir and western hemlock — identical cathedral-and-fog atmosphere, different species. Here's how to keep the redwoods anyway.

The forest

Coast Range old growth

Sitka spruce and Douglas fir to 250 feet. Fog that never fully lifts, moss to the shoulder, sword fern, nurse logs with hemlocks growing out of them in a row.

Sound doesn't carry. Neither does line of sight. Both are mechanics.

The Plantation

The redwoods you wanted

A 1930s Forest Service experimental redwood plantation — coast redwoods planted four hundred miles out of range by men who assumed somebody would come back in a century to measure them.

Ninety-odd years on they are enormous, wrong, and utterly silent. The Firefly camp is inside it. It's real forestry practice, so it survives a player Googling it.

Nestucca Ridge

KVLR 1490

A brick building and a guyed tower above the Nestucca drainage, twenty minutes off Highway 101. Licensed 1961, played country records, carried something else.

Mount Hebo · 3,154 ft

Sundial

A real Air Force radar station — the 689th Radar Squadron, 1956 to 1979, a SAGE air-defence node whose three 140-foot radomes were repeatedly destroyed by Pacific storms. The site was stripped and returned to nature in 1980.

Which is the point. There is nothing on that summit. Everything that survived is underneath it.

On the second tower you asked for

It doesn't fit. Beats one and two alone are an hour, and the Boone scene is now the best scene in the session because the dead FEDRA man turned it from a negotiation into a shared emergency. Cutting Boone to reach 1020 would be trading your strongest material for a rushed dungeon.

Session two ends with the bearing to 1020 and a reason to hurry. Session three is the tower, and it gets a full build. If you'd rather have the tower this Friday, cut beat six — the Plantation — and open session three there instead.

Before Friday Prep checklist

  • Name the sound-bait rule after the player who invented it and announce it in the first five minutes
  • Write Ocampo's name on a card — plus the kid's drawing folded in his breast pocket, if you want it to hurt
  • Print Ellis's note to hand over physically; the crossed-out 14XX matters
  • Read the numbers aloud yourself. Flat, unhurried, no character voice. Practise it once.
  • Count the sixty seconds for real when Sundial demands identification

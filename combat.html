Read this before session one Lethality

This game's damage numbers are enormous relative to Health. A starting survivor has 25 HP. Run this like D&D and you will kill someone in the first fight — not as a dramatic beat, but as an accident.

What this means at the table: combat is a failure state, not the default activity. Wounds never heal on their own and kits can't be looted, only crafted. Reward avoidance loudly and early — the group that sneaks past three Runners should feel like it won something, because it did.

How every roll works Enemies never roll

The player rolls D10 + skill modifier against a number printed on the enemy. Enemies have no dice of their own — they only have DCs. Your whole job in a fight is reading the right DC off the stat block.

Modifiers you will actually use At the table

Cover

+3 Full — wall, pillar
+2 Semi — desk, cabinet
+1 Low — chair, debris

Firearms only. Cover does nothing against a machete. Flanking cancels it entirely.

Overwhelming Odds

Each additional melee attacker on the same PC adds +1 to that PC's Dodge DC — and they roll a separate dodge against every attack.

This is what actually kills people. Three Runners on one survivor is a death sentence; spread the threat.

Darkness

No flashlight means disadvantage on Instinct, Survival, Aiming, Scavenging and Maintenance.

Batteries and basements are a legitimate pacing tool. Use it before you reach for more enemies.

Worn gear

Body Armor −1D8 damage, −3 Elusive
Combat Helmet −1D4 damage, −2 Instinct

Both are real trades. A helmeted PC walks into ambushes.

Grapple

Brutality vs the Infected's Grapple DC. Success means that enemy deals no damage this turn.

Teach this in session one. It's the single best defensive option a melee character has.

Shivs

A stealth kill with a shiv requires no attack roll — pass the Sneak DC to get behind them and the kill is automatic.

They also break after one use. Shiv economy is a real resource decision.

After the hit lands Consequence chain

15+ damage from a human
Roll D8 on the melee or ranged Damage Chart. Melee: smashed hand, fractured wrist, broken arm, cracked ribs, shattered knee, concussion, cracked jaw, gash. Ranged: penetrating wound, grazed, weapon shot, exit wound, headshot, artery hit, bleeding out, ricochet. Most need a Medicine check or a minimum Medicine modifier to clear. Infected attacks do not trigger this — they carry infection instead.
Dropped to 0 by a biter
Roll for the bite. Runner 1-in-6 · Stalker 2-in-6 · Clicker 3-in-6. Then roll location for the clock: neck/face/head = 5–15 minutes; torso/arm/shoulder/hand = 2–6 hours; leg/foot = 12–24 hours; a 10 means immune. The player may keep a bite secret from the rest of the party — offer that privately, in a note.
At 0 Health
Not dead. Down, and out of the fight. The next damage they take kills them outright. This is the tensest moment the system produces — someone has to reach them, and the only thing that helps is a First Aid Kit.
Spores in the air
No gas mask means three turns before automatic infection. Announce the count out loud. It turns a room into a timer.

House rules for a table of five Recommended

This system resolves nearly everything per-character. With five players and a 2.5-hour window, unmodified play spends most of its dice on procedure. These four rulings buy back roughly half an hour a session.

One stealth roll

A nominated point-person rolls Elusive for the whole party against the highest Sneak DC present. Success or failure belongs to the group. Five sequential rolls means someone always fumbles and stealth never happens.

One scavenge roll

One Scavenging roll per lootable area, highest modifier leading, then split the results. Keep individual rolls for anything a single character is doing alone.

Declare, then resolve

Use the handbook's Strategy initiative: the party states its plan and turn order once, then resolves. Faster than rolling for order and it makes them talk tactics.

More bodies, mixed tactics

Encounters read as tuned for three. Add roughly half again — but vary the Tactic line (a Flanker plus a Suppressing Fire shooter changes the geometry more than two extra thugs ever will).

Fast rulings Things the book leaves open

When a player asks…Rule it this way
"Can I attack and move?"Yes. Movement plus one Action, every turn. Movement is free.
"How far can I move?"Anywhere reasonable in the described space. There's no grid — if it needs a roll, it's an Elusive check, not a distance.
"Can I shoot into melee?"Yes, but a natural 1 hits the ally. Say this out loud before they roll.
"I have no weapon."Bare hands, 1D4, Brutality to hit. Improvise from the room — the book expects scavenged bricks and bottles.
"Can I heal them?"Only with a First Aid Kit (10 + your Medicine roll). There is no other healing until someone buys Endure and Survive with Pills.
"Do I get my dodge if I'm surprised?"Yes, always — but apply Overwhelming Odds and any disadvantage. Never remove the roll; removing the roll is how PCs die feeling cheated.
"Can we run away?"Yes, and you should encourage it. Elusive to break line of sight, then a Sneak roll to drop back to Alert. Fleeing is a designed mechanic, not a forfeit.
"How many can attack me at once?"Cap it at three in melee for sanity. Beyond that, extra enemies reposition or attack someone else.

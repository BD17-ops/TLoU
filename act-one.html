The Sundial
Arc

Five sessions, roughly ten weeks. The party lights one relay and learns that somebody has been listening the whole time. The question Act One asks is not "is anyone out there" — it's "what do you owe the people who never answered."

Sessions5 × 2.5 hours
CadenceTwice monthly, Fridays
Span~10 weeks
Pills awarded~210 per player
RulesStandard throughout
Relays lit1 → 2

Act One is built so that the party's own first victory causes its final crisis. Lighting KVLR is the right thing to do and they should feel good about it — and it is also the act that puts a bearing on a FEDRA signals map. Every subsequent broadcast adds another line. By session five the lines cross, and they cross over people the party has come to care about.

That's the engine. Everything else is scenery.

GM only — what Sundial is

Sundial is not a threat. It's a functioning settlement of about two hundred people living in a Cold War–era relay bunker: lights, running water, a greenhouse, a school with actual children in it. They have been listening to the whole surviving network for six years and have never once transmitted, because transmitting is how you get found.

They are, in other words, exactly what the campaign is reaching for — and they will not join the chain, because the chain endangers them. The antagonist of Act One is people who have already achieved the thing you want and won't share it, and who are not wrong to be afraid.

The image that turns this from a negotiation into a moral problem is in session four: a wall in their comms room listing every call sign that ever cried for help on the network. Six years of them. All unanswered, all recorded in a careful hand.

The Signal

Linear · infiltration

Objective: get inside KVLR, find fuel, cable and a key, and put the tower back on the air. Fully built — see the KVLR 1490 session map.

Must establish

  • Combat is lethal and avoidance is the winning move
  • The Ashline exist and have a standing
  • Wren omitted something
  • The Survivor's Journal habit

New mechanics

  • Skill checks, DCs, crits
  • Stealth states and stealth kills
  • Scavenging and loot themes
  • Grapple as a defence

Where it flexes

Cut node 10 entirely if you're short — ending on Sundial's voice is a cleaner cut than a chase, and the escape opens session two beautifully.

Hold the Ridge

Siege · defence

Objective: Wren reaches KVLR with a directional receiver. She needs the tower transmitting for a full night to get a bearing on Sundial — and the FEDRA patrol named in the Ashline ledger saw the tower light up two nights ago. Hold the station until dawn.

Why this session

It inverts session one. Same building, but now they own it and something is coming to take it. Fortifying a space you had to sneak through is enormously satisfying, and it costs you almost no new prep — you already have the map.

New mechanics

  • Crafting — molotovs, nail bombs, trap mines
  • Cover properly, against guns for the first time
  • The 15+ damage charts — humans with rifles
  • Chokepoints, generators, and the fuel clock

The Ashline pay off

Allied: six melee fighters hold the lobby with them. Hostile: a second front, and Boone's people know the building better. Destroyed: nobody but them, and the loading bay is undefended. Session one's choices are worth an entire act structure here.

Ends onWren has a bearing and a direction. The cost is real — lose a named NPC here, not a PC. FEDRA now has the frequency and a second line on the map.

The Hard Road

Open world · travel

Objective: cross roughly ninety miles toward the bearing. This is the session where everyone breathes. Low combat density, high world, and the first real chance for the five of them to be people rather than a tactical unit.

Why this session

The book distinguishes Open World from Linear travel for a reason, and two dungeons back to back will flatten your table. This one is also your light prep Friday — an encounter table does most of the work, so schedule it for a week you're buried at school.

New mechanics

  • Survival checks drive encounters — good and bad
  • Transportation — a horse, if they earn one
  • The infection clock, if someone gets bitten out here
  • A second relay station, optional and skippable

Plant here

They are being followed, and have been for two days. Ivo, a Sundial scout, sent to look at them before anyone decides anything. He is not hiding especially well — he wants to be found on his own terms.

Ends onIvo steps out of the treeline with his hands open and says he's been told to bring five people, blindfolded, or nobody at all.

The Glass House

Social infiltration

Objective: get inside Sundial, and get a look at the transmitter they're sitting on. Almost no combat. They are guests — disarmed, fed, given beds, shown a greenhouse and a classroom — and none of it is a trick. That's what makes it hard.

Why this session

Your table of teachers will remember this one. The tension is entirely social: everyone is kind, the food is hot, there are children doing long division, and the leadership will not let them leave with the location. A velvet cage is more unsettling than a dungeon and costs you nothing in stat blocks.

New mechanics

  • Faction standing as a tracked value, not a vibe
  • Introduce Morality now, if the group wants it — it gives them a lever in a session with no combat
  • No new combat rules at all. Let them rest.

The turn

The comms room. A wall of call signs — every voice that has cried for help on this network in six years, logged in a careful hand, every one of them unanswered. Sundial isn't hiding a monster. It's hiding a choice they make every single day.

Ends onAn offer: stay, join us, stop transmitting. And a condition — you don't leave with what you now know.

Intensity

Who Answers

Climax · the choice

Objective: decide what Sundial is for. The FEDRA column that's been assembling since session two arrives four days out, and the party is the only reason anyone knows it's coming — because the party is the reason it's coming.

The choice

  • Warn and defend — spend everything to save people who wouldn't help you
  • Leverage — Sundial joins the chain or fights alone
  • Take the map and run — six years of call signs is the campaign in one document
  • Open the frequency — broadcast Sundial's location in clear and call every surviving cell. Help comes. So does everything else.

New mechanics

  • Everything at once — this is the exam
  • Optional: if a PC dies or a party member is lost here, this is your natural moment to introduce Trauma as a consequence rather than a rule

What must be true after

The chain has a second light or it doesn't. Every faction has a fixed standing. And the players understand that transmitting has a price — which is the tension the whole campaign runs on from here.

Ends onWhatever they chose, the radio is on, and this time more than one voice comes back.

Progression The only dial you have

There is no XP and no levels — Pills are the entire curve. The awards below are tuned so that every player can afford Life Tier 1 (50 Max Health) before session five, which is what stops the climax being a bloodbath. Teaching curve Don't dump it all in session one

Five players learning a new system need roughly two new mechanics a session, not twelve. This is the order I'd introduce them in — each one arrives in the session that makes it obviously useful.

SESSION 01
  • Skill checks & DCs
  • Stealth states
  • Basic combat & dodge
  • Scavenging
SESSION 02
  • Crafting & throwables
  • Cover against firearms
  • The 15+ damage charts
  • Overwhelming Odds, felt properly
SESSION 03
  • Open World travel
  • Survival-driven encounters
  • Transportation
  • Infection clock (if it comes up)
SESSION 04
  • Faction standing, tracked
  • Morality, optional
  • Nothing new in combat
SESSION 05
  • All of it, under pressure
  • Trauma — only if the fiction earns it

Running it around a teaching job Prep load

Two heavy Fridays

Sessions 02 and 05 need real prep — a wave plan and a defended map for the siege, and a four-way branching climax that has to land. Everything else is light-to-medium.

Session 03 is your recovery

An encounter table does most of the work. If a marking week collides with a session, make it this one — and don't be afraid to move it in the running order.

Session 02 reuses session 01's map

Deliberately. Same building, inverted purpose. It's the cheapest strong session in the arc and it makes session one's geography pay dividends.

Track four things, that's all

Ashline standing · Sundial standing · bearings FEDRA holds · relays lit. Four lines on an index card after every session. Everything else lives in the players' journals.

If it goes sideways Contingencies

If…Then
They refuse to go toward SundialSundial comes to them. Ivo arrives at KVLR in session three instead, with the same offer and more urgency. The arc is about the meeting, not the journey.
They kill Boone in session oneSession two is harder and lonelier, and that's the correct consequence. Give the Ashline survivors a grievance that pays off in Act Two rather than punishing the siege further.
A PC dies before session fiveBring the new character in through the faction the party is currently closest to. A Sundial defector in session four is an especially good entry.
Sessions run longCut the second relay station in session three and the escape in session one. Never cut the Boone conversation or the wall of names — those are the arc's two load-bearing scenes.
The group loves the talking more than the fightingLean in. Expand session four to two sessions and push the climax to session six. A table of teachers finding out they enjoy negotiation is a good problem.
They side with Sundial and stop transmittingTake it seriously — it's a legitimate reading of the theme. Act Two becomes about whether a safe two hundred can stay safe, and the FEDRA column still arrives.
